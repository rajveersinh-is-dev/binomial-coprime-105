\begin{abstract}
We investigate Erd\H{o}s problem \#376: are there infinitely many $n$ such that
$\binom{2n}{n}$ is coprime to $105=3\cdot 5\cdot 7$?
We record an elementary proof for a single prime, a computational search up
to $3^{22}$, and the observation that the problem remains open for the triple
of primes $(3,5,7)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}
By Kummer's theorem, a prime $p$ does not divide $\binom{2n}{n}$ iff every
base-$p$ digit of $n$ is $<p/2$.

\section{Single-prime lemma}
For any prime $p$, $n_k=(p^k-1)/2$ gives infinitely many $n$ with
$p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}$.

\section{Computation}
Exact search (see \texttt{src/search.py}) found 14 values of $n\le 3^{22}$ with
$\gcd(\binom{2n}{n},105)=1$, the largest being $59548401$.

\section{Status}
The infinitude for the triple $(3,5,7)$ is unresolved.
